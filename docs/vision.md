# Kobold Quest: vision (draft)

*Step 1.1 of the roadmap. Answers from Joakim (creative director) are in.*

## In one sentence

A cozy, funny kobold adventure for your phone: walk a Ruby-sized land with friends, fight little tactical battles,
and help a village that didn't bother to choose.

## What it is

- **Platform:** the phone's browser, held sideways (joakimskar.no), with a desktop and Claude-page version from the same code.
- **Genre:** an overworld adventure in the shape of Pokémon Ruby (towns, routes, key items, a rival, a villain team),
  with small turn-based grid battles instead of monster catching.
- **Pack:** Pip and up to three of the seven companions (a pack of four), each with a class, talents, a subclass and gear.
- **Together:** one shared world for everyone: see each other, chat, yip, fight side by side, duel, trade at the market.

## Pillars (proposed)

1. **Cozy and funny.** Kobolds are small, brave and silly. Jokes over grimness.
2. **Phone-first, short sessions.** Anything worth doing fits in a coffee break.
3. **Better together.** Other players make the world feel alive; nobody is required to play with others.
4. **A story with a quiet heart.** The Hatching: not choosing lets others choose for you. Shown, never preached.
5. **Small choices in battle matter.** Positioning and timing, not grinding.

## What it is not

- Not a monster-catching game, not a grind, not pay-to-win, not political.

## Decisions (Joakim, 16 Oct 2026)

- **Audience:** friends first, public later. Polish it for our circle; open up once it's good. Nothing should stop it
  from growing later (accounts, server, moderation).
- **Sessions:** both. Every sitting can end with a small goal reached in 5–15 minutes, but there is enough to keep going
  for an evening. In practice: short hops between fountains, sparks broken into small steps, longer optional areas.
- **Heart of the fun:** all four equally: exploring, tactical battles, playing together, story and jokes. No pillar
  may be traded away for another; every town should touch at least three of them.
- **Art:** Joakim draws it. The game's look comes from his drawings; the code's job is to bring them in cleanly
  (see the style guide, step 1.5, and the tech plan, step 1.7: an art pipeline from his drawings to the game).
  He draws in **Paint.NET with a pen on a touch screen**: PNG with transparency, layers in .pdn files.
