# The story: the Hatching

**Cozy first, to look at; a proper story underneath.** On the surface: small kobolds, snacks, goats, shiny things. Under
it, a story about **taking part in a democracy**, never named: no votes, parties, taxes or turnout. The eggs really are
ugly, and Pip's "why bother?" is treated as fair. Nobody lectures. Pip changes his mind because of what he sees.

**How the theme is carried.** Everyone has a life first. Towns are cultures with their own history, customs and ways of
deciding things, some good, some broken; the theme comes out of those, sideways. Most villagers have nothing to do with
it. A player who only wants cozy kobolds should never feel taught; a player who looks closer should find the whole story.
(The tables below name the themes for us, the writers. The game never does.)

## Kobaldia makes buns together (draft, 19 Oct 2026)

The story starts in **the capital, in the middle of the map**: marble streets, a castle, and the Great Nest in the
castle yard. The capital is **Hearthspire** (every town's hearth was first lit from the fire in its tower).
**Burrowhome is dropped:** Pip and Bib live in Hearthspire. The whole country works together to make ember-buns:

| who | what they do | where (draft) |
|---|---|---|
| **Mossbacks** | farming kobolds: wheat (the old "Mossback" monsters get a new name: **Nettlenose** scouts and slingers) | the green fields (Mossmere, Pebblebrook's farms) |
| **millers** | grind the wheat | river mills (Pebblebrook) |
| **bakers** | bake in the volcano's heat | Emberreach |
| **riders** | carry flour and buns everywhere, riding **raptors** | the roads, from a crossroads town (Thistledown) |
| every other town | one ingredient or tool each: copper ovens (Copperhollow), salt (Saltspray), yeast from the marsh (Glimmerfen), ice cellars (Frostwick), honey (Sunhaven) | |

Three layers of why: kobolds are cold-blooded, so ember-buns (baked with volcano fire, they stay warm for hours) are
what keeps them going; no town can make one alone, because each has something the others lack; and that's why the
guardian's job is to keep the whole chain fair, and why a spark from every town's hearth is needed to hatch one.

## The villains (decided 19 Oct 2026; replaces the bandits in the opening)

**Bib, Pip's best friend, is the antagonist.** He is indifference with a face. Pip does care: he walks to the nest
meaning to warm an egg. Bib talks him out of it ("Buns are fine. Nothing ever changes. Why bother?"), and Bib has
been saying the same to the whole village for weeks. Hardly anyone comes.
- *Why is Bib like that?* Once he tried: he asked the whole village to help fix the old mill, and nobody came.
- *Why did nobody come?* Everything was fine. The old guardian kept it fine, so nobody ever had to care.
- *Why does that matter?* Good times made the village forget why the hatching exists, and Bib is what that forgetting
  looks like. He isn't evil. He's comfortable, a bit hurt, and very persuasive.

**The three village guards** are the only ones who care, and they care a lot. They stand watch every night, and they
believe that the ones who work hardest should get the most buns: all of them, ideally. They aren't wrong that they
work hard. The old guardian was the only one who ever made them share, so they want a guardian who agrees with them.
They warm the shiny egg, three embers in a nearly empty nest, and win fairly.
- *Why only the guardian could stop them:* they hold the spears. A guardian's job is to keep the strong from taking
  everything.

**Glitterbeak** hatches and agrees with whoever warmed him: buns to the guards first. Nobody cheated. The village just
stayed home.

## Premise

Once in a lifetime Burrowhome hatches a **dragon** to be the kobolds' **guardian**. A guardian looks after everything:
the fields, the mill, the hearths, everything buns come from. Three dragon eggs sit in the **Great Nest**, and every
kobold who wants to warms the egg they like best with an **ember**. The warmest egg hatches.

**Buns are the analogy for what a society makes and shares.** Old Mallow's ember-buns are what everyone gets from
the way things are run. Each egg is a different way of running it:

| egg | what that dragon would do | the catch |
|---|---|---|
| **sprouty** (green, a leaf on top) | ploughs new fields: more buns for everybody | everyone helps with the harvest |
| **sleepy** | keeps everything just as it is | nothing gets better either (Mallow's old oven) |
| **shiny** (on a pile of gold crumbs, with a ribbon) | promises "the finest buns" | buns go to its hoard first; kobolds get the crumbs. Snatchclaw's bandits planted it |

**Pip is content, so he keeps his ember.** He gets two buns every morning and can't eat more than two: "Everything's
fine. Why bother?" It isn't the player's choice; it's who Pip is at the start. Most of the village feels the same.
Snatchclaw's bandits sit by the shiny egg all night with a sack of embers, and out comes **Glitterbeak**, the kobolds'
new guardian, who guards *his hoard*. By breakfast every batch goes to the hoard first. Pip: "But I didn't choose any
of this." Mallow: "No, dear. Somebody did."

**The bandits are kobolds.** Only kobolds have embers, and every kobold gets exactly one. Nobody cheats: the "sack of
embers" is one ember from each of Snatchclaw's crew, carried together and all warming the same egg. They aren't
stronger or smarter than the village; they simply all showed up for the same thing while most of the village stayed
home. (Cheating would teach a different lesson, "the system is rigged", which is the opposite of ours.) The friendly
stranger at the nest is a kobold from the hills, Snatchclaw's cousin.

An old rhyme says a guardian who hatched wrong can hatch again, if a **spark from every town's hearth** relights the Great
Nest. A hearth spark is a town's say; only a town that has sorted out its own trouble will give one.

## The world underneath: three layers of why

Told cozily: through a children's rhyme, murals in the town halls, old kobolds' stories and things lying around. Never
as a lecture or a tragedy.

**1. Why warm eggs with embers?** Kobolds are small and cold-blooded. A dragon egg needs far more warmth than any one
kobold has, so it only hatches from many embers together. An ember is a bit of hearth fire carried in a clay cup.

**2. Why does it matter who warms it?** A hatchling becomes like whatever warmed it. Warmed by a whole village, it
belongs to the whole village; warmed by a few, it belongs to those few. That's simply how dragons work, and it's why
the Old Guardian looked after everyone and Glitterbeak looks after the bandits.

**3. Why do kobolds have the custom at all?** Long ago Kobaldia had a **dragon king**. He hatched his own heirs, and
kobolds were his hoard-keepers, polishing gold in the dark. One night every town carried fire to his mountain together,
and the king left and never came back. From that night's fire came every town's **hearth**, which is why a hearth spark
is a town's say, and why the Great Nest needs a spark from every town to be lit. Afterwards the kobolds learned layer 2,
and made the hatching: a guardian warmed by everyone, so no dragon would ever again answer to only a few.

**What follows, without anyone saying it:**
- **Why nobody bothers any more.** The last hatching was a lifetime ago, and nobody alive remembers the dragon king. He's
  a nursery rhyme ("the king who ate the gold"). Peace made people forget why the custom exists. "It's fine like it is"
  comes from history, not from one grumpy kobold.
- **Why kobolds love shiny things.** An old habit from the hoard-keeping days. Harmless, mostly. It's what Snatchclaw
  pulls on.
- **Snatchclaw.** His family were the dragon king's hoard-masters, rich and important once. He doesn't want chaos; he
  wants the old days back, with himself as the dragon's favourite.
- **The shiny egg.** The dragon king's last egg, hidden by Snatchclaw's family for generations. Glitterbeak is the old
  king's grandchild. But warmth makes a dragon, not blood, so the second hatching can still give him a good heart.
- **Each town decides in its own way**, from its own past: Copperhollow's shift circle round the hearth, Thistledown's
  two guild councils, Frostwick's fort meeting, Glimmerfen's gossip at the well. Some work and some are broken, and the
  player compares them without being told to.

## The town that pushes back: Frostwick

Frostwick doesn't care for the hatching. "Warm an egg and walk away? Pah. Down here we build." Every winter the whole
town builds the snow fort together, and argues about it the whole time. It's slow, loud and nobody's favourite plan.
Pip turns up to watch and grumble about their walls, and gets nothing but a snowball: *you didn't build, so you don't
get a say about the walls.* Once he helps, his plan loses the vote, he builds the winner anyway, and now when he
complains, they listen, because it's his fort too.

What's underneath: **ownership.** Taking part is what earns you a real opinion. Watching from the side doesn't let you
grow. Frostwick never mentions eggs, and is right about something the egg story alone can't show.

## The opening, as played

1. **Pip's want comes first:** the intro says he loves one thing above all, Old Mallow's ember-buns.
2. Elder Mumble gives him an ember. At the nest Pip hears the crowd, keeps his ember, and curls up by the warm nest for
   a nap (he's cold-blooded).
3. **He sees the hatching:** a CRACK at dawn, a glittering little dragon, the stranger's sack full of empty ember cups,
   a dozen cheering bandits. Glitterbeak gulps Glint's spoon and is carried off down the east road. Pip: "...Huh."
4. The Elder tells the rhyme of the sparks. Pip: "Sounds like a job for somebody else." "Mm. Somebody else is a very
   busy kobold."
5. **Pip's own reason to go:** Old Mallow has no flour, because the bandits put a toll on Pebblebrook, where the mill
   is. No buns. Pip: "Right. Where's Pebblebrook?" He goes for buns, not sparks; the sparks find him on the way.
6. Wizzle waits at the east road: Grandma saw the dragon fly over. "I'm only going for buns." "Buns are a GREAT reason!"
7. **Glitterbeak bans buns.** At the hatching he eats Mallow's whole tray (they glow a little); by breakfast the
   bandits say every ember-bun in Kobaldia is his. A rule for everybody, made by a dragon hardly anybody chose: the theme,
   felt in Pip's stomach. Mallow slips him her last bun, hidden in her hat.
8. Turnip gives a sack of flour with Pebblebrook's spark. Mallow bakes two in secret: "That's the last of them, until
   somebody sorts out that dragon." Pip: "...Then I suppose somebody had better sort out the dragon." The small want
   turns into the big quest.
9. **Three ember-buns in the whole game** (heal everything, fill all MP; can't be bought, gifted or sold). After the
   second hatching, buns are legal again and Mallow sells them.

Witch Yarrow is an optional visit in Whisper Woods: her grandmother's story about the king who ate the gold, and two ethers.

### The rival
**Tikka** kept her ember on purpose: "Not picking is my way of saying no." When Glitterbeak hatches she's furious, mostly
at herself, and sets out to fix it alone and faster. She stands for **doing it all yourself**. She battles Pip three
times (Thistledown, Frostwick, Mossmere) and gets stronger each time, but she can't relight a nest alone. Her excuse is
the last one Pip answers. The fourth bowl ("none of these, try again") gives her an honest way to say no that counts,
and she brings her ember. She joins as a ranger.

guardian is borrowed, not owned. She grounds Glitterbeak and, at the end, asks Pip to help choose the next eggs.

## Tone rules for the theme

- **Agree with the doubter first.** Burrowhome really is lovely, the eggs really are ugly, and the guilds really are
  annoying.
- **Cozy to look at.** Every scene works as a cute scene first. The meaning is a second layer for those who look.
- **Show, never tell.** Nobody says "vote", "election", "party" or "politics", and no character explains the lesson.
- **Wrong is allowed.** Pip, Glint and Cinder are wrong in likeable ways and learn by seeing what happens.
- **Losing is part of it.** Fennel's egg lost and Pip's fort plan lost; both still matter.
- **Funny first.** Every lesson comes wrapped in a joke, a goat or a sandwich.

## How it's built

- **Flags** in `s.q` (internal names kept from the first draft): `moot` (stage), `vote` (always `'skip'`: Pip keeps his ember),
  `seals` (sparks), `pb`, `ch`, `miners`, `lantern`, and later `th`, `sh`, `er`, `fw`, `gf`, `bc`.
- **Barriers** (`BARRIERS` in `data/overworld.js`): toll gates `Y` (until `bc`), the Route 7 rockslide `R` (until `fw:3`),
  the dark tunnel `Z` on Route 8 (until `lantern`).
- **Town villagers** stand at spots from each town's square (`townSpot`) or inside its houses.
- **Fights:** Grubb (`tollboss`), Snipe (`collector`); later three Tikka rival fights (a party that levels with the story), Snatchclaw at Emberreach (he flees) and Brindlecove (final boss).
- **Frostpeak:** a small new area above Route 13, reached with the warm boots.
- **Tests:** `test/logic/hatching.test.js` plays it through and checks every barrier and villager.
